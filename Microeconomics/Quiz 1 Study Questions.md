# Microeconomics — Quiz 1 Study Questions

These original practice questions are based on the Quiz 1 topics listed in Moodle and the course materials for Weeks 1 and 3. Try answering the questions before checking the suggested answers.

## Practice questions

1. What does **scarcity** mean in economics? Give an example of how scarcity could affect a hotel's decision.

2. What are the four **factors of production**? Give one hotel-related example of each.

3. A hotel has CHF 100,000 and chooses to buy new guest-service technology. Its next-best option was an advertising campaign expected to generate CHF 42,000 in benefit. What is the hotel's **opportunity cost**?

4. A hotel is considering adding one extra housekeeping shift. It is expected to generate CHF 300 in additional revenue and cost CHF 220. Should the hotel add the shift according to the **marginal principle**? Explain.

5. Give one example of a positive incentive and one example of a negative incentive for hotel staff. Describe one possible unintended effect of an incentive.

6. Water is essential for life, while diamonds are not. Why can diamonds still have a higher price? Use the ideas of **scarcity** and the value of an additional unit in your answer.

7. Explain the difference between **microeconomics** and **macroeconomics**. Then classify each question:
   - How might a new café affect coffee prices in its local area?
   - How is unemployment changing across a country?

8. What three basic questions must every economic system answer?

9. A guest pays CHF 200 for a hotel night. Describe how the payment and the hotel stay appear in the **circular flow model**. Which flow consists of money, and which consists of goods or services?

10. In the Moodle production possibilities frontier (PPF) example, moving from point C to point D adds one conference event and gives up 20 leisure stays. What is the opportunity cost of that additional conference event?

11. What does it mean when a production combination is **on**, **inside**, or **outside** a PPF?

12. A hotel hires more employees. Would this normally cause a movement along its PPF or shift the PPF? Explain how this differs from an increase in demand for hotel rooms.

13. Can a PPF tell a business which option is most profitable or fairest? Why or why not?

14. A hotel has a limited budget and must choose between improving guest services and buying more advertising. Explain how you would use **scarcity**, **opportunity cost**, and **marginal benefit** to analyse the decision.

## Suggested answers

1. Scarcity means that available resources are limited compared with people's wants. A hotel might have to choose between spending its limited budget on staff training or renovating rooms.

2. **Land:** natural resources or the site/building location. **Labour:** employees' work, skills, and knowledge. **Capital:** equipment, buildings, or technology used to provide services. **Entrepreneurship:** organising resources and taking business risks.

3. CHF 42,000: the benefit of the next-best alternative the hotel gives up. Opportunity cost is the value of the best forgone alternative, not the total value of every rejected option.

4. Yes, based on the information given. The marginal benefit is CHF 300 and the marginal cost is CHF 220, so the additional benefit exceeds the additional cost by CHF 80.

5. A positive incentive could be a bonus for excellent guest service. A negative incentive could be a penalty for repeated lateness. A possible unintended effect is that a speed-based bonus encourages staff to rush and service quality falls.

6. Price does not depend only on how important an item is overall. It is also affected by scarcity and the value people place on an additional unit. Water is essential but often widely available; diamonds are scarcer.

7. Microeconomics studies individual consumers, businesses, and specific markets. Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole. The café question is microeconomics; unemployment across a country is macroeconomics.

8. **What** should be produced? **How** should it be produced? **Who** should receive what is produced?

9. The guest's money payment goes to the hotel as revenue. In return, the hotel provides a service to the guest. Money flows in the opposite direction from goods, services, and productive resources.

10. The opportunity cost is 20 leisure stays: that is what the hotel gives up to produce one additional conference event.

11. **On the PPF:** possible and productively efficient. **Inside the PPF:** possible, but resources are not being used to their full productive capacity. **Outside the PPF:** unattainable with current resources and technology.

12. More employees normally increase productive capacity and shift the PPF outward. Higher demand does not by itself change what the hotel can produce; it may change which point, or output mix, the hotel chooses.

13. No. A PPF shows feasible combinations, capacity, trade-offs, and opportunity costs. By itself, it does not show prices, revenue, costs, profitability, or fairness. A production choice can be efficient but unprofitable.

14. Identify the scarce resource—in this case, the budget. Compare the additional benefit and cost of each option. The opportunity cost of the chosen option is the benefit of the best option the hotel gives up.
